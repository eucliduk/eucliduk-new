Euclid is a flagship space telescope launched by the European Space Agency in July 2023. While its main mirror is only about half the size of the Hubble Space Telescope's at 1.2 m, Euclid can image a much larger part of the sky in one go — observing more of the Universe in a few days than Hubble will ever see.

Euclid is based at L2, a dark and calm spot four times further from Earth than the Moon, following Earth's orbit around the Sun. It is creating a map of the deep Universe over six years, covering all of the sky not obscured by our own galaxy.

On board Euclid are two instruments: the visible light instrument, VIS, is among the largest digital cameras ever to go into space. University College London (UCL) contributed to its design and build. A second camera, NISP, captures infrared light that usually gets blocked by Earth's atmosphere.

Scientists at the University of Edinburgh lead the UK's involvement in Euclid's data analysis and host the UK's Science Data Centre, processing huge amounts of data ready for astronomers to make discoveries.

Big Data

The Science Ground Segment

Scientists at the University of Edinburgh lead the UK's involvement in Euclid's data analysis and host the UK's Science Data Centre, processing huge amounts of data ready for astronomers to make discoveries.

The UK has played a key role in the conception and production of Euclid. The production of Euclid's imager for visible light, VIS, is led by the UK. The UK leads the gravitational lensing data analysis that will turn images of galaxies made with VIS into maps of dark matter. The UK also has prominent roles on committees and managerial boards across the Euclid Consortium.
